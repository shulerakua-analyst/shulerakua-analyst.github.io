Learning Architecture

Learn → Apply → Practice → Review → Grow

The Academy is built around doing tax work, explaining judgment and preserving the evidence behind the answer—not just consuming lessons.

LearnSource-linked rules, concepts and terminology.
ApplyGuided examples, calculations and return flow.
PracticeWorkpapers, case packets and controlled variants.
ReviewExplain reasoning, identify errors and evaluate another approach.
GrowAdvance from task competence to connected tax judgment.
Compartmental Capstones

Competence without one giant final project

Capstones stay aligned to the skill being tested, while connected assignments and an advanced residency can build broader tax-department judgment.

I

Return / Tax Competency

Tax-family and return-specific capstones such as SALT, 1040, 1065, 1120-S, 1120, payroll, provision or accounting skill areas.

II

Connected Tax Competency

Paired work such as Federal + State, Payroll + Federal, Sales Tax + Nexus, Assets + Federal/State, or Notice + Amendment.

III

Tax Department Residency

An advanced experience concept that can span multiple assignments once its governed source framework is complete.

Multi-Case Learning Standard: major capstones can use up to three equivalent controlled variants for learner choice, randomized assignment, cohort breakouts, repeat attempts, compare-and-contrast debriefs and cross-review.
Breakout Case Lab

Same competency. Different fact pattern.

Case banks are designed to make practice realistic and repeatable without changing the mapped learning outcome.

Learner Choice

Choose from approved variants during developmental practice.

Random Assignment

Use controlled assignment when the goal is assessment integrity.

Cohort Breakouts

Different teams work parallel cases and reconvene to compare reasoning.

Cross-Review

Teams review another case outcome to build reviewer and manager judgment.

Difficulty Tiers

Foundation, Practitioner and Advanced variants can increase complexity while preserving the core competency.

Repeat Practice

A learner can retry with a new fact pattern rather than memorizing the previous answer.

Source Governance

Curriculum is traceable to its source

Published learning assets remain connected to the underlying course, outcome and governed source record so source changes can be traced forward to affected lessons, workpapers and assessments.

Traceability standard: governed source → knowledge point / outcome → lesson → workbook, case, lab or job aid → assessment → learner evidence.
Source-protection rule: when approved curriculum is incomplete, the Academy preserves the source-pending shell instead of inventing tax content to make a track appear finished. Manitoba RST remains the current one-lesson example.
Credential Architecture

One Academy, distinct completion standards

The structure separates Harbor & Hive professional training from future CPE-eligible and IRS continuing-education pathways, allowing the appropriate metadata, assessment and completion evidence to be layered onto eligible course versions.

Professional Training

Practical skills, workpapers, cases, reviewer development and job-performance evidence.

CPE-Ready Metadata

Objectives, audience, level, prerequisites, field of study, learning time, assessment and reviewer/source records.

IRS CE Assessment Mode

Separate question-bank and completion logic can be applied to eligible course versions without changing the underlying Academy architecture.
